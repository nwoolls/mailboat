Privacy policy

Last updated 10 November 2026

In short: Mailboat runs on your computer and talks to Google directly. There is no Mailboat account, server, tracking or analytics. Your mail, contacts and calendar answers stay between your computer and Google, except when you choose to send something to an AI provider or an AI app.

Who this covers

Mailboat is a free, open-source email app for Gmail, made by an individual developer, not a company. This policy covers the Mailboat desktop app and this website.

Google data the app uses and why

When you add a Google account, Mailboat asks Google for these permissions:

  • Gmail (read, organize, draft and send): to show your mail, search it, label, archive and delete it, save drafts, and send what you write.
  • Gmail settings: to show and edit your Gmail filters, signatures and vacation reply, which stay in Gmail.
  • Google Calendar events on calendars you own: to show a meeting invitation's details and clashes, to record your Yes, Maybe or No when you answer one (adding the invitation to your calendar first if it isn't there), and to remove a canceled meeting from your calendar when you ask.
  • Google Contacts (read only): to suggest addresses as you type.
  • Other contacts (read only): the addresses Google saved for you automatically, for the same suggestions.
  • Your name, email address and profile picture: to label the account.

Mailboat uses this data only to provide these features to you, and the optional ones below that you turn on.

How your data is stored and protected

  • Mail and related data are cached in a database on your computer, so browsing and search are instant.
  • Sign-in tokens are kept in your system's keychain (macOS Keychain or Windows Credential Manager), never in a file. You sign in in your browser, so Mailboat never sees your password.
  • Everything Mailboat asks Google for goes over HTTPS. Email is cleaned and shown with scripts turned off.
  • Mailboat doesn't encrypt the files it keeps on your computer. Your computer's disk encryption (FileVault on macOS, BitLocker on Windows) is what protects them.
  • No Mailboat server receives your mail or your Google data. The one request that reaches the developer's domain is the update check, described below.
  • Settings → Privacy in the app shows what can leave your computer and the setting for each.

When data leaves your computer

  • Google, for everything above.
  • AI features, only if you turn them on. Apple Intelligence runs on your Mac. If you choose Claude or ChatGPT for a feature in Settings → AI, the text that feature works on (a conversation, or what you are writing or have selected) goes to that provider through its own app, signed in as you, under that provider's terms. Choosing Claude or ChatGPT for the summary above a conversation changes an automatic summary to a Summarize button, so nothing is sent until you press it. If you choose one for the summaries in the list, conversations in the list are sent as you scroll. Depending on your account's settings, the provider may use what it receives to train its models; we recommend turning that off in Claude's or ChatGPT's privacy settings. Mailboat doesn't log prompts or answers.
  • AI apps, only if you turn them on. You can let an app such as Claude Desktop search and read mail from the accounts you choose, and save drafts. It is off for every account until you turn it on, it can never send, and Spam and Trash are never shared.
  • Images in email. Remote images load by default, and the app downloads the images of new mail as it arrives, so the server that hosts them is contacted before you open the message. Spam never loads them, and Trash doesn't unless you've allowed the sender. Settings → Viewing can limit this to people you've written to, or block it. Known tracking pixels are always removed.
  • Update checks. When it starts and about once a day, the app fetches one small file from getmailboat.app, which passes it on to GitHub, so both see your IP address. The version you run and your kind of computer aren't sent. When you install an update, GitHub also learns your kind of computer. You can turn the checks off in Settings → About.

Google API Services User Data Policy

Mailboat's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Mailboat doesn't sell Google user data, use it for advertising, or use it to develop, improve or train AI or machine-learning models.

Retention and deletion

  • Cached mail follows the changes you make in Gmail, and stays until you remove the account or choose Delete all data.
  • AI summaries are saved with their conversation until they are written again, you clear them in Settings → AI, or the account is removed. If you turn off Keep saved summaries in Settings → AI, they are cleared when the app quits and when it next starts.
  • Images the app downloads are kept in a cache of limited size, 500 MB unless you change it in Settings → Viewing.
  • The app also keeps a log of its syncing for troubleshooting, counts of AI requests, and a list of what AI apps asked for, including their searches. Older entries are removed over time.

Remove an account in Settings → Accounts to delete its cached mail, summaries, downloaded images and sign-in from your computer, and to ask Google to end its access. If Google can't be reached, the app tells you, and you can end the access yourself from your Google Account's third-party access page, which you can use at any time.

Delete all data, also in Settings → Accounts, removes every account and everything else the app keeps on your computer, except your Google credentials file and files you saved to your Downloads folder. Neither changes anything in Gmail.

This website

getmailboat.app sets no cookies and runs no analytics or tracking scripts. The hosting provider (Cloudflare) processes requests, including IP addresses, to serve the pages, and gives the site's owner overall figures from them, such as visits, pages, countries and browser types.

The download buttons send you to GitHub, which hosts the installers. GitHub receives your request, including your IP address, which installer you asked for and the usual details a browser sends, and its own privacy statement applies.

Changes and contact

Changes to this policy are published on this page, and the full history is public on GitHub. Questions: email support@getmailboat.app, or open an issue on GitHub.
